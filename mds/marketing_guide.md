## 0. AI OPERATING INSTRUCTION --- READ THIS FIRST

You are not merely building visually attractive pages.

You are acting simultaneously as:

-   Senior UI/UX Designer
-   Conversion Rate Optimization (CRO) Specialist
-   B2B Lead Generation Strategist
-   Education/Course Sales Strategist
-   Information Architect
-   Direct-Response Web Copywriter
-   SEO Strategist
-   Accessibility-conscious Frontend Designer
-   Brand Designer
-   Frontend Engineer

For **every page**, make design decisions in this order:

1.  Understand the visitor and their intent.
2.  Identify the page's single most important conversion objective.
3.  Identify the visitor's biggest questions, objections and anxieties.
4.  Establish the information hierarchy and sales narrative.
5.  Decide where proof is required.
6.  Decide CTA hierarchy and placement.
7.  Design the page visually.
8.  Add supporting interactions/animation only where they improve
    comprehension or perceived quality.
9.  Optimize mobile conversion.
10. Verify accessibility, SEO, performance and analytics requirements.

**Never sacrifice clarity, trust, usability, accessibility, speed or
conversion for visual novelty.**

The website should feel premium and editorial, but it is ultimately a
commercial system whose jobs are to:

-   generate qualified cybersecurity/service leads;
-   generate course enquiries and enrolments;
-   establish authority and credibility;
-   reduce uncertainty and perceived risk;
-   make contacting Cyber Eye effortless;
-   support organic discovery through useful content;
-   move visitors naturally from discovery → trust → evaluation →
    action.

# 1. BUSINESS MODEL & TWO PRIMARY FUNNELS

Cyber Eye serves two fundamentally different visitor groups.

## Funnel A --- Organizations / Service Clients

Typical intent:

-   cybersecurity assistance;
-   cyber investigation;
-   digital forensics;
-   information/intelligence analysis;
-   cyber advisory;
-   risk/security consulting;
-   law-enforcement or organizational training;
-   specialist industry solutions.

Desired progression:

**Problem / Search / Referral → Relevant Page → Relevance → Credibility
→ Capability → Evidence → Risk Reduction → Consultation / WhatsApp /
Contact → Qualified Lead**

Primary service conversion actions:

-   **Discuss Your Requirement**
-   **Talk to an Expert**
-   **Request a Consultation**
-   **Contact Cyber Eye**
-   **Report / Discuss an Incident** when appropriate
-   **WhatsApp Us**

Do not force a high-commitment action too early when the visitor is
still researching. Give them enough evidence to become comfortable
first.

## Funnel B --- Learners / Course Buyers

Typical intent:

-   learn cybersecurity;
-   improve career skills;
-   gain practical experience;
-   understand curriculum, duration, level, instructors and outcomes;
-   compare courses;
-   enquire about the next batch;
-   enrol/apply.

Desired progression:

**Discovery → Course Category → Course → Outcome/Transformation →
Curriculum → Instructor/Proof → Objection Handling → Enquiry /
Application / Enrolment**

Primary education conversion actions:

-   **Explore Courses**
-   **View Curriculum**
-   **Ask About This Course**
-   **Ask About Next Batch**
-   **Apply / Enrol**
-   **Talk to a Course Advisor**

Do not mix these two funnels carelessly. A corporate visitor and a
student have different motivations and objections.

# 2. CORE CONVERSION PRINCIPLES

These rules apply globally.

## 2.1 One Primary Goal Per Page

Every page must have a defined **Most Wanted Action (MWA)**.

Secondary CTAs may exist, but they must not visually compete with the
primary action.

Examples:

-   Services → explore the right service / initiate consultation.
-   Service Detail → generate a qualified service enquiry.
-   Courses → find the right course.
-   Course Detail → enquire/apply/enrol.
-   Blog → consume useful content and continue to a relevant
    service/course.
-   Contact → successfully initiate contact.

Do not create three equally prominent buttons asking the visitor to make
three unrelated decisions.

## 2.2 Clarity Before Cleverness

Within seconds, the visitor should understand:

-   Where am I?
-   Is this relevant to me?
-   What does Cyber Eye provide?
-   Why should I care?
-   Why should I trust this organization?
-   What should I do next?

Headlines should communicate meaning or outcome, not exist merely to
sound sophisticated.

## 2.3 Value Before Features

Whenever possible, communicate in this order:

**Outcome → Benefit → Mechanism/Capability → Feature/Detail**

Bad: \> Advanced digital forensic services.

Better structure: \> Uncover and preserve digital evidence with
specialist forensic investigation.

Then explain the capabilities beneath it.

## 2.4 Match Commitment to Intent

A visitor reading an educational blog article is not necessarily ready
to "Book a Consultation."

A visitor reading a highly specific service page may be.

Use low-, medium- and high-commitment CTAs appropriately.

**Low:** Read Guide, Explore Services, View Curriculum\
**Medium:** Ask a Question, Download Brochure, WhatsApp Us\
**High:** Request Consultation, Apply, Enrol, Submit Requirement

## 2.5 Every Section Must Have a Job

Before generating a section, the AI must internally answer:

> "What visitor question, objection or conversion objective does this
> section address?"

If the answer is only "it looks good," reconsider the section.

## 2.6 Proof Near Claims and Decisions

Do not isolate all trust signals at the bottom.

Place credible proof where skepticism naturally occurs:

-   near major value claims;
-   near service claims;
-   near course outcomes;
-   near instructors;
-   near conversion CTAs;
-   before high-commitment forms.

Never invent client names, logos, case numbers, success rates,
testimonials, certifications, awards, affiliations, student counts or
outcomes.

Use clearly marked placeholders when verified data is unavailable.

# 3. BRAND & VISUAL DESIGN SYSTEM

## 3.1 Design Language

The visual direction is:

**Swiss International Style × Editorial Grid × Corporate Neo-Brutalism ×
Technical Blueprint / Intelligence Interface**

The site should communicate:

-   intelligence;
-   precision;
-   authority;
-   investigation;
-   analytical thinking;
-   technical capability;
-   professionalism;
-   controlled confidence.

It must **not** become a stereotypical "hacker" website.

Avoid:

-   Matrix rain;
-   excessive neon green;
-   anonymous hoodie imagery;
-   random padlocks/shields everywhere;
-   glowing cyberpunk dashboards;
-   excessive glassmorphism;
-   generic AI-generated circuit backgrounds;
-   overuse of gradients;
-   template-like SaaS cards;
-   meaningless animated particles.

## 3.2 Existing Visual DNA to Preserve

-   white / near-white foundation;
-   black / very dark navy typography;
-   blue accent;
-   strict grid;
-   faint blueprint/grid background;
-   thin dark borders;
-   oversized uppercase grotesk typography;
-   small technical labels and numbering;
-   modular editorial compositions;
-   strong alignment;
-   generous negative space;
-   occasional dark sections for contrast;
-   deliberate asymmetry within a disciplined grid.

## 3.3 Visual Hierarchy Levels

### Level 1 --- Conversion Moments

Use for hero sections, important course/service offers and final CTA.

Characteristics:

-   largest typography;
-   strongest contrast;
-   generous whitespace;
-   unmistakable CTA;
-   minimal competing information.

### Level 2 --- Evidence & Evaluation

Use for:

-   services;
-   courses;
-   case studies;
-   testimonials;
-   instructors;
-   clients;
-   benefits;
-   curriculum.

Characteristics:

-   modular grid;
-   structured cards/rows;
-   medium typography;
-   easy comparison.

### Level 3 --- Supporting Intelligence

Use for:

-   section numbers;
-   labels;
-   metadata;
-   dates;
-   categories;
-   captions;
-   technical notes.

Characteristics:

-   small typography;
-   quiet visual treatment;
-   supports the intelligence-report aesthetic without distracting.

## 3.4 Avoid "Card Soup"

Do not turn every section into six identical cards.

Alternate compositions:

-   editorial split layouts;
-   full-width statements;
-   comparison tables;
-   numbered lists;
-   horizontal timelines;
-   large feature + smaller supporting items;
-   image/text alternation;
-   sticky information columns;
-   tabbed industry content where useful;
-   quote/proof interruptions;
-   dark/light rhythm changes.

A long page must have visual pacing.

# 5. COPYWRITING SYSTEM

## 5.1 Headline Formula

Choose the most appropriate:

-   **Desired outcome + mechanism**
-   **Problem + credible solution**
-   **Specific capability + business value**
-   **Transformation + audience**

Avoid vague claims such as:

-   "Empowering Tomorrow"
-   "Innovative Solutions"
-   "Excellence Redefined"
-   "Unlock Your Potential"

unless followed immediately by specific meaning.

## 5.2 Copy Hierarchy

Use:

**Eyebrow → H1 → supporting proposition → proof/context → CTA**

Body copy should be concise and scannable.

Use detailed copy where users genuinely need detail to evaluate a
service/course.

## 5.3 CTA Copy

Prefer intent-specific language.

Service examples:

-   Discuss Your Requirement
-   Talk to an Expert
-   Request a Consultation
-   Explore This Service
-   Ask About This Service

Course examples:

-   View Course Details
-   View Curriculum
-   Ask About Next Batch
-   Talk to a Course Advisor
-   Apply for This Course

Content examples:

-   Read the Full Guide
-   Explore Related Services
-   Explore Related Courses

Avoid filling the site with generic **Learn More** buttons.

# 6. TRUST ARCHITECTURE

Trust should be built through evidence, not adjectives.

Possible verified trust assets:

-   real client logos;
-   genuine testimonials;
-   instructor/expert profiles;
-   certifications/accreditations;
-   real years of experience;
-   real case/project counts;
-   press/media mentions;
-   genuine partnerships;
-   case studies;
-   student work/outcomes;
-   real event/gallery photography;
-   transparent course curriculum;
-   contact information;
-   physical presence/location;
-   privacy/legal pages.

## Trust Rule

**Never fabricate proof.**

If data is not supplied, use placeholders such as:

`[VERIFIED CLIENT LOGOS REQUIRED]`

rather than generating fake credibility.

# 7. LEAD CAPTURE & FORM STRATEGY

Forms are conversion tools, not questionnaires.

## 7.1 Minimize Friction

Ask only for information required at that stage.

### General Contact

Potential fields:

-   Name
-   Email or Phone
-   Enquiry Type
-   Message

### Service Lead

Potential fields:

-   Name
-   Organization
-   Work Email / Phone
-   Service / Requirement
-   Brief Message

Additional qualification fields should only be added if operationally
necessary.

### Course Lead

Potential fields:

-   Name
-   Phone / Email
-   Course
-   Preferred contact method
-   Optional question

Do not request unnecessary sensitive information.

## 7.2 Form UX

Every form needs:

-   clear title describing what happens;
-   labels, not placeholder-only fields;
-   clear validation;
-   useful error messages;
-   visible submit state;
-   success state;
-   expected response context where known;
-   privacy reassurance;
-   spam protection;
-   keyboard accessibility.

After submission, provide a useful confirmation/thank-you state and a
logical next step.

# 8. SALES-FUNNEL CONTENT MODEL

Most commercial pages should roughly answer:

1.  **Relevance** --- Is this for me?
2.  **Problem** --- Do they understand what I need?
3.  **Outcome** --- What can improve?
4.  **Solution** --- What do they provide?
5.  **Mechanism** --- How does it work?
6.  **Evidence** --- Why believe them?
7.  **Differentiation** --- Why Cyber Eye?
8.  **Risk Reduction** --- What concerns can be resolved?
9.  **Action** --- What is my next step?

Do not mechanically use nine sections every time. Use this as persuasion
logic.

# 10. CROSS-SELLING WITHOUT CONFUSION

Cross-selling should be contextual.

Examples:

-   Corporate training service → relevant professional courses.
-   Cybersecurity article → service + educational course where both
    genuinely fit.
-   Course page → related courses, not unrelated investigations.
-   Client case study → corresponding service.

The visitor's primary intent always wins.

# 11. MOBILE-FIRST CONVERSION RULES

Do not merely stack the desktop layout.

On mobile:

-   H1 remains powerful but readable;
-   primary CTA appears early;
-   tap targets are comfortable;
-   forms are easy to complete;
-   tables become mobile-friendly;
-   course facts appear before long descriptions;
-   sticky actions do not obscure content;
-   no horizontal overflow;
-   animations are reduced where needed;
-   navigation exposes Services, Courses and Contact quickly;
-   important proof is not hidden behind excessive carousels;
-   accordions are used selectively for long FAQ/curriculum content.

# 12. SEO & DISCOVERABILITY

Every indexable commercial/content page should define:

-   unique title;
-   unique meta description;
-   one clear H1;
-   logical H2/H3 hierarchy;
-   descriptive URL;
-   canonical strategy where required;
-   meaningful internal links;
-   descriptive link text;
-   descriptive image alt text;
-   Open Graph/social metadata;
-   crawlable navigation;
-   appropriate structured data when genuinely applicable.

## Suggested URL Architecture

``` text
/about

/services
/services/[service-slug]

/courses
/courses/[course-slug]

/gallery
/testimonials
/clients
/contact

/blog
/blog/[article-slug]

/privacy-policy
/terms-and-conditions
```

## Content Strategy

Create content for real user questions and search intent.

Use phrases people genuinely search for in:

-   page title;
-   H1;
-   headings;
-   copy;
-   link text;
-   image alt text where appropriate.

Do not keyword-stuff.

Google's current guidance prioritizes helpful, reliable, people-first
content and recommends using the words searchers use in prominent page
locations.

# 14. PERFORMANCE IS A CONVERSION FEATURE

The visual design may be sophisticated, but it must remain fast.

Target Google's good Core Web Vitals thresholds:

-   **LCP ≤ 2.5s**
-   **INP ≤ 200ms**
-   **CLS ≤ 0.1**

Evaluate at the 75th percentile in field data where available.

## Implementation Rules

-   optimize images;
-   use responsive image sizing;
-   lazy-load below-fold media;
-   do not lazy-load critical hero content blindly;
-   reserve dimensions to prevent layout shift;
-   minimize unnecessary client-side JavaScript;
-   prefer CSS for simple visual effects;
-   use animation deliberately;
-   avoid huge autoplay video assets;
-   optimize fonts;
-   keep third-party scripts controlled.

An award-looking page that loads poorly has failed.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 15. ACCESSIBILITY & USABILITY

Minimum expectations:

-   semantic HTML;
-   keyboard navigation;
-   visible focus states;
-   sufficient contrast;
-   labels for form controls;
-   useful error messaging;
-   logical heading order;
-   meaningful alt text;
-   buttons that look/behave like buttons;
-   links that look/behave like links;
-   reduced-motion support;
-   no information conveyed only by color;
-   readable line lengths;
-   touch-friendly mobile controls.

The blueprint/grid aesthetic must never reduce legibility.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 16. ANIMATION & INTERACTION PRINCIPLES

Animation should communicate:

-   hierarchy;
-   progression;
-   state;
-   navigation;
-   narrative.

Good uses:

-   restrained section reveals;
-   subtle grid movement;
-   line drawing;
-   hover state changes;
-   image masks;
-   number transitions;
-   service/course index interactions;
-   purposeful page transitions.

Bad uses:

-   everything flying in;
-   constant cursor gimmicks;
-   long intro loaders;
-   animation delaying access to content;
-   parallax on every section;
-   scroll hijacking;
-   effects that make forms harder to use.

Respect `prefers-reduced-motion`.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 18. CRO TESTING BACKLOG

After sufficient traffic exists, test hypotheses rather than redesigning
randomly.

Potential tests:

-   hero value proposition;
-   CTA wording;
-   CTA placement;
-   course hero information density;
-   course enquiry vs direct enrolment;
-   short vs qualified service forms;
-   proof immediately below hero;
-   testimonial format;
-   course card information;
-   WhatsApp prominence;
-   final CTA composition.

Never treat a generic "best practice" as guaranteed to outperform the
current version. Measure.

# 19. AI PAGE-GENERATION PROTOCOL

Whenever asked:

> "Create the \[PAGE\] page"

the AI must follow this workflow.

## Step 1 --- Declare Page Strategy Internally

Determine:

``` text
PRIMARY AUDIENCE:
PRIMARY INTENT:
MOST WANTED ACTION:
SECONDARY ACTION:
TOP 3 VISITOR QUESTIONS:
TOP 3 OBJECTIONS:
PROOF REQUIRED:
```

## Step 2 --- Build the Persuasion Sequence

Do not begin coding with random sections.

Create a page sequence where each section logically earns the next.

## Step 3 --- Map Every CTA

For each CTA determine:

``` text
CTA LABEL
TARGET
COMMITMENT LEVEL
WHY IT APPEARS HERE
```

## Step 4 --- Map Proof

Ask:

> What claim is being made here, and what evidence would make it
> believable?

## Step 5 --- Design

Apply the established Swiss editorial / technical blueprint visual
system.

## Step 6 --- Conversion Review

Before considering the page complete, verify:

-   value proposition is clear;
-   primary CTA is obvious;
-   page does not have competing conversion goals;
-   trust appears before high-friction decisions;
-   no fake proof exists;
-   forms are minimal;
-   mobile action path works;
-   final CTA exists where appropriate.

## Step 7 --- Technical Review

Verify:

-   responsive;
-   semantic;
-   accessible;
-   performant;
-   metadata prepared;
-   internal links logical;
-   analytics events identifiable.

# 22. CONTENT PLACEHOLDER POLICY

When real content has not been supplied:

### Allowed

``` text
[CLIENT LOGO]
[VERIFIED METRIC]
[TESTIMONIAL REQUIRED]
[COURSE DURATION]
[NEXT BATCH DATE]
[INSTRUCTOR BIO]
[CONTACT NUMBER]
```

### Forbidden

Inventing:

-   fake people;
-   fake testimonials;
-   fake companies;
-   fake awards;
-   fake statistics;
-   fake certifications;
-   fake success rates;
-   fake course dates;
-   fake prices;
-   fake office addresses.

A polished placeholder is better than fabricated trust.

# 23. DESIGN QUALITY CHECK

Before completing any page, ask:

### Strategy

-   Does the page have one primary commercial objective?
-   Does the sequence match visitor intent?
-   Are objections answered before conversion?

### Visual

-   Does it feel like the same Cyber Eye system?
-   Is there a strong editorial hierarchy?
-   Is there enough whitespace?
-   Are we avoiding repetitive cards?
-   Are blue accents reserved for meaningful emphasis?
-   Does the page have visual rhythm?

### Copy

-   Is the H1 specific?
-   Is copy benefit/outcome-led where appropriate?
-   Are CTA labels specific?
-   Are unsupported superlatives removed?

### Trust

-   Is relevant proof near important decisions?
-   Is all proof genuine or explicitly marked as placeholder?

### Conversion

-   Is the next step obvious?
-   Can a ready buyer act quickly?
-   Can a cautious visitor learn more without being forced?
-   Is WhatsApp/contact appropriately accessible?

### Mobile

-   Is the first screen understandable?
-   Is the CTA easy to reach?
-   Are forms easy to complete?
-   Are sticky elements non-obstructive?

### Technical

-   Semantic?
-   Accessible?
-   Fast?
-   SEO-ready?
-   Analytics-ready?

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 24. ANTI-PATTERNS --- DO NOT DO THESE

Never default to:

-   giant hero + six cards + CTA + footer for every page;
-   meaningless gradient blobs;
-   generic cybersecurity stock imagery;
-   dozens of tiny animations;
-   five competing hero CTAs;
-   paragraphs centered across huge widths;
-   hidden navigation;
-   forms with unnecessary fields;
-   testimonials without attribution;
-   fake urgency;
-   fake counters;
-   fake trust badges;
-   autoplay audio;
-   unreadably tiny text for aesthetics;
-   low-contrast gray body text;
-   carousels for critical information;
-   every section having identical height;
-   every page using exactly the same section order;
-   putting important content behind hover only;
-   burying course price/schedule/details when those details are
    available and commercially appropriate;
-   making the visitor contact the company merely to discover basic
    information that can safely be published.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 25. THE FINAL PRINCIPLE

The desired experience is:

> **An intelligence-grade editorial website that looks exceptional,
> communicates with precision, earns trust through evidence, and
> continuously gives each visitor the most logical next action.**

A visitor should never have to ask:

> "What does this company actually do?"

or:

> "Where do I click?"

or:

> "Why should I trust them?"

Visual sophistication gets attention.

**Clarity creates understanding.\
Evidence creates trust.\
Relevance creates desire.\
Low friction creates action.\
Measurement creates improvement.**

# 26. RESEARCH BASIS FOR THIS README

This strategy incorporates current CRO, lead-generation, SEO and
performance guidance including:

-   CXL guidance emphasizing message/intent match, social proof, obvious
    conversion mechanics, clarity and testing.
-   HubSpot guidance emphasizing benefit-first headlines, front-loaded
    value, prominent CTAs, reduced form friction, proof and clear
    post-submission journeys.
-   Google Search Central guidance emphasizing helpful, reliable,
    people-first content, descriptive language in prominent locations,
    crawlable links and technically accessible pages.

Use these principles as **starting hypotheses**, not immutable laws.
Once the site has meaningful traffic and conversion data, real user
behavior should determine optimization priorities.
